Hiding takes a screen name. Getting caught takes one trace.

Online anonymity and accountability under the Cybercrime Prevention Act (RA 10175).

Anonymous Doesn't Mean Untraceable

Artist
Stefany Dizon
Medium
Digital poster, made in Canva
Subject
Online anonymity and accountability under the Cybercrime Prevention Act (RA 10175).

Words of The Artist

500 to 600 words

For our poster, our group wanted to talk about something a lot of people don't really think about. It's the idea that you're "anonymous" online. A lot of us act like no one can trace what we do on the internet. Especially if we use a fake name or a different account. That's actually the main message of our poster. We want people to realize that everything they do online leaves some kind of trace. Even small things like sending a message or logging into an account. So being "hidden" online doesn't really mean you're safe from being found.

When it comes to ethical theories we can connect this to a few ideas. If we think about utilitarianism cybercrimes like scams or hacking really do hurt people. Victims lose money. They feel scared. They get embarrassed. So we should think about the actual harm these actions cause instead of just assuming "walang makakaalam." If we look at deontology some actions are just wrong no matter what. Like stealing someone's identity or deceiving people online. Even if the person doing it doesn't get caught. In virtue ethics it's basically about having good character even when no one is watching. That includes how we act online.

Freedom of expression is also connected to this topic. People do have the right to speak up or stay anonymous online. Especially if they're scared to use their real identity. But that freedom has limits. It doesn't give anyone the right to harass lie about or scam other people. This is where laws like the Cybercrime Prevention Act (RA 10175) come in. It clearly states that hacking identity theft and online fraud are punishable. Even if the person thinks they're anonymous.

This also connects to intellectual property. A lot of cybercrimes involve stealing someone else's information data or work. Again people usually do this because they think they won't get caught.

As a group we believe people deserve privacy and freedom online. But that doesn't mean they're free from responsibility. Our poster's message is simple. The internet might feel anonymous. But it was never really untraceable.
