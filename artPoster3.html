Fair Use

Artist
Clarenz Leycano
Medium
Digital poster, made in Canva
Subject
Fair use, freedom of expression and intellectual property rights, told through a falling winged figure surrounded by glitching copyright symbols.

Words of The Artist

500 to 600 words

This poster is inspired by the story of Icarus, a human who sought to fly high with wings made from mere bird feathers and beeswax, but in disregard of Daedalus’s warnings, his desperation to reach the sun would soon melt his wings and lead to his fall into the sea.

The wings itself is the symbol for creativity as well as freedom of expression. One may soar high when mustering their creativity and freely expressing themselves through their literary and creative works. Gaining their wings correlates to having freedom to explore the possibilities of what may seem like a limitless sky or a whirlpool of inspiration and ideas.

The bottom half of the poster showcases a variety of colorful light rays to symbolize the mesmerizing effect of freedom of expression, allowing them to stand out. Similar to how light passing through a prism displays multiple colors of light, the light reflected out by the wings shows how intellectual properties can encourage creativity, innovation, and development.

The top half of the poster displays a much darker tone, showcasing the limit or restrictions created by laws like RA 8293, the Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines, which sought to protect creative activity. It also symbolizes the zone where overstepping your freedom takes a toll on your work, similar to how Icarus’s wings melted upon flying too high. It can also symbolize how strict copyright, trademark, and patent laws can limit the freedom of those who pursue greater creativity.

The act of soaring too high is the main theme and message of the poster. A person may be creative and free to express their ideas, but there is a limit on how they can utilize the inspirations and ideals that they have. With their ideas, they could soar in the sky, fly as high as they wish, and shine their light above all those who view their work. But sometimes, it’s either they take advantage of that freedom and use as many inspirations as possible and ideas from other people too much, their wings no longer represent their own, or how unfair and unbalanced laws can affect those who seek creative freedom by making them fear the heights and bounds of freedom of expression. Which is why when Icarus’s wings melted, it silenced his pride of flying high, similar to how copyright can legally strip the creator’s rights from their work.

The poster may have two different meanings: one, which is that overly strict IP laws can limit the creativity of people, and the other, which is that people who do not respect such laws will eventually bring their work to an end, but it still showcases how imbalance can risk people’s faith in their own freedom of expression. This is why Icarus was warned by his father, Daedelus, to fly a middle course, where he is not too exposed to the sun and not too low, where he’ll fall to the sea. The middle zone represents the middle ground of creative expression and intellectual property protection laws, which is why people should be free to express their ideas while free from the shackles of the rules imposed upon them.

Fair use or warning against misuse?

To start off I would say using a celestial figure whose wings are disintegrating under copyright symbols captures the tension between creative freedom and intellectual property restrictions. It visualizes the hazard of flying "too close to the sun" when reusing protected works without understanding legal boundaries.

The pixelated, corrupted glitch artifacts on the feathered wings contrast well with the classical painting aesthetic, visually bridging traditional art with modern digital media and online reproduction was amazing. But it is slightly unclear whether the poster is advocating for fair use, warning against the misuse/overreach of copyright strikes, or cautioning creators that fair use isn't an absolute shield and also stacking letters vertically weakens immediate readability. A strong horizontal title would maintain punch without bifurcating the subject.
